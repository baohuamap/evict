\section{Evaluation Plan}
\label{sec:evaluation}

The full evaluation targets more than 50,000 alerts across Juliet and CWE-Bench-Java, spanning controlled ablations and deployment-oriented settings. Table~\ref{tab:datasets} summarizes the role of each dataset. We will compare EVICT against state-of-the-art neuro-symbolic baselines, most notably \textbf{IRIS}~\cite{li2025iris}. Specifically, we leverage the IRIS infrastructure to generate CodeQL databases and alerts for the 120 CVEs in \textbf{CWE-Bench-Java}, providing a direct head-to-head comparison between EVICT's selective symbolic escalation and IRIS's LLM-assisted specification inference on identical static analysis findings. Additional baselines include evidence-free prompting, evidence-conditioned prompting without calibration, LLM4FPM, LLM4PFA, AdaTaint-style neuro-symbolic validation, and a non-LLM Random Forest classifier over SARIF metadata (rule identifier, severity, path depth, slice size) to establish the contribution of LLM reasoning over traditional heuristics. Primary metrics are precision, recall, F1, coverage, selective risk, and ECE, together with the false-negative rate on auto-dismissed alerts, abstention on true vulnerabilities, latency, token cost, and solver utilization.

The study is organized around four concrete, testable questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Precision--coverage tradeoff}: Does EVICT achieve $\geq 85\%$ precision at $\geq 80\%$ coverage across Juliet, NASCAR, and CWE-Bench-Java, and does this hold across analyzers (SpotBugs, Infer, CodeQL)?
\item \textbf{Calibration under shift}: Does ECE remain below 0.1 under cross-project and cross-analyzer transfer, and does within-project or Mondrian-style calibration recover coverage when it degrades?
\item \textbf{Symbolic verification payoff}: Does conditional symbolic escalation correct a statistically significant number of LLM errors per unit of solver time, and does the false-negative rate on auto-dismissed alerts remain acceptably low?
\item \textbf{Cost asymmetry sensitivity}: How does the optimal operating point shift as $\gamma$ and the $\alpha/\beta$ cost ratio vary, and does asymmetric label-conditional calibration (Theorem~\ref{thm:label_conditional}) outperform symmetric split-conformal calibration at the same target coverage?
\end{enumerate}

The main ablations isolate evidence quality (slice-only vs.\ slice+flow+constraints), calibration method (raw confidence, temperature scaling, Bayesian binning, conformal), symbolic verification scope (none, SMT-only, full JPF/KLEE), rejection policy (symmetric vs.\ asymmetric thresholds), and shift-aware recalibration strategy (global vs.\ project-aware vs.\ Mondrian). Our target operating regime is at least 85\% precision at 80\% coverage with a low false-negative rate among auto-dismissed alerts, explicit class-conditional coverage when asymmetric calibration is enabled, and limited cross-project degradation. The planned user study will measure the end-to-end developer time delta between reviewing an EVICT-triaged alert (with rationale and evidence) versus a raw static analysis alert, directly quantifying whether the 10--20 minute manual triage cost is reduced. We also plan to report the engineering cost of setting up JPF/KLEE environments for target repositories in NASCAR and CWE-Bench-Java, as solver harness configuration is a known practical barrier in real-world Java and C++ projects.
