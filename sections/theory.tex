\section{Theoretical Guarantees}
\label{sec:theory}

The formal results make EVICT safe to deploy as a selective triage aid. They are not the empirical novelty of the paper; they define what is guaranteed when the system accepts an alert. All guarantees below apply to the \emph{pre-escalation} conformal predictor under exchangeability. Symbolic escalation is a post-calibration audit stage.

\begin{theorem}[Cost-aware finite-sample excess risk]
\label{thm:risk}
Partition calibration data into an independent grid-construction split $D^{(G)}$ and evaluation split $D^{(E)}$. Let $\mathcal Q_G$ be the finite set of feasible TP/FP threshold pairs induced by $D^{(G)}$, $\hat q\in\arg\min_{q\in\mathcal Q_G}\hat R_E(q)$, and $q_G^\star\in\arg\min_{q\in\mathcal Q_G}R(q)$. Define $C_{\mathrm{TP}}=\max(\alpha_{\mathrm{cost}},\gamma_{\mathrm{abs}})$ and $C_{\mathrm{FP}}=\max(\beta_{\mathrm{cost}},\gamma_{\mathrm{abs}})$. If $m^{(E)}_{\mathrm{TP}}$ and $m^{(E)}_{\mathrm{FP}}$ are the TP and FP counts in $D^{(E)}$, then, conditional on $D^{(G)}$, with probability at least $1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}
R(\hat q)-R(q_G^\star)\le
2\pi_{\mathrm{TP}}C_{\mathrm{TP}}\sqrt{\frac{\log(4|\mathcal Q_G|/\delta)}{2m^{(E)}_{\mathrm{TP}}}}
+2\pi_{\mathrm{FP}}C_{\mathrm{FP}}\sqrt{\frac{\log(4|\mathcal Q_G|/\delta)}{2m^{(E)}_{\mathrm{FP}}}}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:risk} is a structural justification for label-conditional threshold optimization. It avoids treating the LLM as a finite-VC classifier; the optimized object is a low-dimensional threshold rule over calibrated scores. The bound decomposes by label, so the scarce or expensive stratum controls risk in asymmetric security settings.

\begin{theorem}[Split-conformal validity]
\label{thm:conformal}
For any nonconformity score $A(x,y)$, if the calibration alerts and test alert are exchangeable, the split-conformal prediction set satisfies
\begin{equation}
\Pr[Y\in\hat C(X)]\ge 1-\alpha_{\mathrm{conf}}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

This is the standard conformal guarantee~\cite{vovk2005}. EVICT's contribution is the software-engineering decision rule layered on top: singleton sets produce committed TP/FP decisions, while empty and multi-label sets become abstentions.

\begin{theorem}[Label-conditional asymmetric conformal validity]
\label{thm:label_conditional}
Let $\hat q_{\mathrm{TP}}$ and $\hat q_{\mathrm{FP}}$ be label-specific conformal quantiles computed from TP and FP calibration strata with target miscoverage rates $\alpha_{\mathrm{TP}}$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{FP}}$. Define
\[
\hat C_{\mathrm{asym}}(x)=\{y\in\{\mathrm{TP},\mathrm{FP}\}: A(x,y)\le \hat q_y\}.
\]
Under exchangeability within label strata,
\begin{align}
\Pr[Y\in\hat C_{\mathrm{asym}}(X)\mid Y=\mathrm{TP}]&\ge 1-\alpha_{\mathrm{TP}},\\
\Pr[Y\in\hat C_{\mathrm{asym}}(X)\mid Y=\mathrm{FP}]&\ge 1-\alpha_{\mathrm{FP}}.
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

Choosing $\alpha_{\mathrm{TP}}<\alpha_{\mathrm{FP}}$ directly encodes the cost asymmetry of missed vulnerabilities: the system is more conservative about excluding TP than FP. Operationally, this bounds the auto-dismissal false-negative rate at the calibration layer.

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite-sample optimal thresholds]
\label{thm:exact_opt}
Given ordered TP and FP nonconformity scores, the globally optimal asymmetric threshold pair over the empirical selective-risk objective can be found by exhaustive search over at most $(m_{\mathrm{TP}}+1)(m_{\mathrm{FP}}+1)$ candidate pairs after sorting, in $O(m^2)$ time.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:exact_opt} means asymmetric threshold selection is not heuristic tuning. Once the cost model is fixed, the calibration split induces a finite optimization problem with an exact solution.

\paragraph{Project shift and sequencing.}
The conformal guarantee is exact under exchangeability. Cross-project transfer weakens this assumption because alerts cluster by project, framework, analyzer, and rule. Practical mitigations include within-project calibration, Mondrian-style conditioning on analyzer or project group~\cite{romano2020}, and rolling recalibration. None restores exact validity under arbitrary shift, but all preserve the intended behavior: when calibration assumptions are less trustworthy, EVICT should predict less often rather than more aggressively. If a deployment wants calibrated confidence after symbolic escalation, it must recalibrate on post-escalation outputs.
