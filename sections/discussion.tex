\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The confidence-signal bottleneck.}
EVICT's calibration layer is mathematically standard; the hard part is producing a score that contains uncertainty information. Study~2 shows the bottleneck directly: R1-Distill-14B yields 36\% unanimous vote-share and ECE 0.335, while DS-Coder-V2-Lite yields 98\% unanimity and ECE 0.620 on the same 247-alert Juliet subset. Conformal calibration cannot recover information absent from the score. The design implication is that alert-triage research should optimize the confidence-producing interaction, not only the classifier.

\paragraph{Two paths to calibration.}
The measured studies identify two routes to lower ECE. Reasoning models produce diverse confidence because different self-consistency samples disagree when evidence is ambiguous. Frontier-lite models can achieve lower ECE through higher base precision even when vote-share is degenerate, as Claude Haiku 4.5 does in Study~2 with 47.4\% precision, 90\% unanimity, and ECE 0.373. The projected RQ5 outlook estimates that frontier reasoning models would be valuable precisely because they may combine both mechanisms.

\paragraph{Prompt design as an intervention.}
The contrastive prompt result is stronger than the cross-model pattern because it holds the model fixed. For Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite on the 247-alert Study~4 subset, contrastive reasoning reduces ECE from 0.555 to 0.337 and unanimity from 84.6\% to 16.2\%. Crucially, an FP-Hunter variant that inverts the decision prior---assuming FP unless all vulnerability preconditions are met---simultaneously increases precision by 13.2\,pp (38.9\%~$\to$~52.1\%) and reduces ECE by 75\% (0.555~$\to$~0.137). This shows that the same prompt-level intervention can fix confidence degeneracy \emph{and} improve precision, the two properties EVICT needs for deployable selective prediction. The trade-off is over-conservatism: FP-Hunter induces 78\% LLM-level abstention, reducing coverage. Whether this is acceptable depends on the cost asymmetry: in security contexts where a missed vulnerability is far costlier than a manual review, precision at the expense of coverage is the preferred operating point. Future work should explore tunable abstention thresholds that interpolate between the aggressive default prompt (high recall, low precision, degenerate confidence) and the conservative FP-Hunter (high precision, low coverage, informative confidence).

\paragraph{The code-specialization paradox.}
Study~3 shows that code-specialized models can be better base classifiers even when they are worse confidence estimators. DS-Coder-V2-Lite has the worst Study~2 confidence quality but the highest Study~3 true-negative count on real CodeQL alerts. A practical architecture is therefore two-stage: use code-specialized models to recognize safe code patterns and a reasoning or contrastive-prompted model to generate a calibrated uncertainty signal.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The conformal proof-of-concept uses 247 Juliet alerts from three CWE classes, and the real-world validation covers 549 CodeQL alerts from 45 of 201 CWE-Bench-Java projects. Juliet is favorable: it is synthetic, balanced by construction, and has 98.7\% complete SARIF paths in the earlier SpotBugs study. CWE-Bench-Java is more realistic but Java-only and highly imbalanced. Symbolic escalation remains limited by Java string constraints: Z3 returns \texttt{UNKNOWN} on 8.1\% of symbolic invocations in the characterization study, motivating deeper Java PathFinder integration. RQ5 frontier numbers are extrapolations bounded by the stated ranges and were not run end-to-end because full frontier-model evaluation with $k_{\mathrm{sc}}=5$ was outside budget. Finally, conformal validity assumes exchangeability; cross-project shift requires within-project calibration, Mondrian conditioning, or rolling recalibration.

\paragraph{Ongoing and future work.}
We are extending the artifact in four directions: richer confidence signals from token-level log probabilities and semantic consistency, a direct IRIS head-to-head on identical CWE-Bench-Java CodeQL alerts, full JPF-based symbolic escalation for Java path feasibility, and execution of the released RQ5 frontier-model harness by groups with sufficient API budget. For weakly labeled corpora such as D2A and NASCAR~\cite{d2a2020,nascar2022}, future versions should combine confident learning~\cite{northcutt2021}, noise-transition modeling~\cite{goldberger2017}, and manual validation of sampled labels.
