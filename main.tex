\begin{abstract}
Static analyzers are essential in secure software engineering, yet their practical value is constrained by false-positive rates of 40--90\% and manual triage costs of 10--20 minutes per alert. Recent LLM-based triage systems condition on analyzer evidence, but they still rely on uncalibrated confidence and forced binary decisions. This is unsafe in the lite-model regime: in our zero-shot Juliet baseline over five lite LLMs and 87+ CWE classes, accepted-alert precision saturates at 42.2--59.9\% and expected calibration error (ECE) remains above 0.35.

We introduce \textbf{EVICT} (Evidence-Conditioned Investigation with Calibrated Triage), a framework that casts alert triage as cost-sensitive selective prediction. EVICT extracts SARIF-derived evidence, applies schema-guided LLM verification, converts raw confidence into conformal prediction sets, and commits to TP or FP only on singleton sets; empty and multi-label sets abstain and may be routed to symbolic escalation. The conformal guarantee applies to the pre-escalation selective predictor, while symbolic verification is reported as a separate post-calibration audit layer.

Across real measured studies, we uncover the confidence-signal bottleneck: on a 247-alert Juliet conformal study spanning CWE-89/78/190, a 14B reasoning model produces only 36\% unanimous self-consistency votes and ECE 0.335, while instruction-tuned and code-specialized models produce 85--98\% unanimity and ECE 0.51--0.62. Holding the model fixed, a contrastive prompt for Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite reduces unanimity from 84.6\% to 16.2\% and ECE from 0.555 to 0.337, showing that prompt design can substitute for model scale in producing calibratable confidence. On 549 real CodeQL alerts from 45 CWE-Bench-Java projects, DS-Coder-V2-Lite achieves the highest true-negative count despite the worst Juliet confidence quality, revealing a code-specialization paradox. These results position calibrated abstention and confidence-signal design as the key levers for deployable LLM-based static-analysis triage.
\end{abstract}
