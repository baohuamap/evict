\begin{abstract}
Static analysis tools are widely deployed in secure software development but suffer from false-positive (FP) rates of 40--90\%~\cite{johnson2013,christakis2016}, with manual triage costing 10--20 minutes per alert~\cite{tencent2023}. Recent LLM-based triage systems improve precision by conditioning on analyzer evidence~\cite{llm4fpm2024,llm4pfa2024,buglens2024}, yet they treat model confidence heuristically, force binary classification without principled abstention, and do not examine whether the confidence signal itself is reliable for selective prediction.

We present \textbf{EVICT} (Evidence-Conditioned Investigation with Calibrated Triage), which reframes alert triage as cost-sensitive selective prediction~\cite{elyaniv2010,geifman2017}: the system accepts alerts only when calibrated evidence supports a reliable decision and defers uncertain cases to human review. EVICT extracts structured evidence from SARIF output, applies schema-guided LLM reasoning, calibrates confidence via split-conformal prediction~\cite{vovk2005,angelopoulos2021}, and invokes symbolic verification selectively on abstained alerts. We prove finite-sample conformal validity for the pre-escalation predictor and derive excess-risk bounds with label-specific convergence rates.

Across six LLMs spanning three training paradigms---reasoning, instruction-tuned, and code-specialized---we uncover a previously unreported finding: \emph{the quality of self-consistency confidence depends more on training objective than on model size}. Reasoning models produce diverse vote-share distributions that yield well-calibrated ECE, enabling effective conformal abstention, while instruction-tuned and code-specialized models exhibit near-unanimous vote-share that renders calibration ineffective. On CWE-Bench-Java~\cite{cwebench2023}, code-specialized models achieve the highest FP rejection despite the worst confidence quality, revealing a code-specialization paradox. We project that frontier reasoning models will be the first model class where conformal calibration delivers practically significant precision gains, establishing a roadmap for deployable LLM-based triage.
\end{abstract}
